    Assessment¶

    How you are graded, how to submit, and what happens when things go wrong.

    Breakdown¶

    Component Weight Timing Format
    Assignments (7, one per chapter) 20% Weeks 4, 6, 7, 10, 12, 14, 16 Take-home; theory + code
    Mid-semester examination 30% Week 8 2 h, closed book
    Final examination 50% Week 18 3 h, closed book
    Total 100%

    Per-assignment weight: ~3% each (7 × 3% = 21%; the actual split is announced with each assignment to reflect the size of the task — the total is exactly 20%).

    Assignments (20%)¶

    What to expect¶

    Each assignment covers one chapter and combines theory with computation:

    • ~60% theory. Analytical problems similar to the tutorial problems, but longer and requiring a full solution — derivation, justification, and interpretation of the result.
    • ~40% implementation. A Python / Java / C# program that implements an algorithm from the chapter, runs it on a specified problem, and reports the results. Code must be your own; libraries such as scipy.optimize are allowed only when the assignment explicitly permits them.

    Rubric (per assignment)¶

    Criterion Weight What earns full marks
    Correctness of final answers 40% All answers correct and complete, including units and interpretation
    Rigour of derivations 20% Each step justified; no hidden assumptions; notation consistent
    Computational implementation 20% Code runs, produces correct results, is readable and commented
    Interpretation and discussion 15% Results placed in context; limitations and edge cases discussed
    Presentation 5% Clear structure; plots labelled; references cited

    Submission¶

    • Format. A single .pdf report (typeset with LaTeX, Markdown → PDF, or Word) plus the source code in a .zip bundle. The PDF must also contain the code listings (or an appendix with the source).
    • Naming. A<n>-<lastname>-<student-id>.pdf and A<n>-<lastname>-<student-id>.zip.
    • Channel. Upload to the course Moodle/LMS assignment dropbox.
    • Deadline. 23:59 local time on the due date. Late submission loses 10% of the maximum mark per calendar day; after 3 days, the submission scores zero.

    Mid-semester examination (30%)¶

    • Format. 2 h, closed book, no calculators beyond a basic scientific one.
    • Content. Covers Chapters 1–3 in full. Weighting is approximately 30% / 40% / 30% across the three chapters — reflecting the amount of material in each.
    • Structure. Typically 4–6 questions, mixed theory and computation: state a definition or theorem; derive a result; solve a small problem by hand; interpret a numerical result.
    • Sample papers. Past mid-semester papers are available on the LMS; the solutions are discussed in the tutorial in week 7.

    Common pitfalls¶

    • Confusing necessary and sufficient conditions (FONC vs. SOSC).
    • Forgetting to check convexity before claiming a stationary point is a global minimum.
    • Sign errors in the KKT multipliers — the sign convention matters.
    • Wrong condition-number intuition — a small condition number is good.
    • Copying a formula without stating the assumptions — most formulas in Chapter 3 (penalty, barrier, augmented Lagrangian) require specific conditions (feasibility, strict feasibility, rank conditions).

    Final examination (50%)¶

    • Format. 3 h, closed book, same calculator policy as the mid-semester.
    • Content. Covers the whole syllabus, with approximately 40% on Chapters 1–3 and 60% on Chapters 4–7 (matching the split of teaching hours).
    • Structure. Typically 6–8 questions. Theory and computation as in the mid-semester, with longer multi-part problems.
    • Emphasis. Chapters 4–7 are examined more heavily because they contain most of the applied material — random walks, shot noise, cyclostationarity, sampling, spectral factorization, LMMSE, Wiener, Kalman, Markov chains, M/M/1, M/M/s.

    What to bring¶

    • Non-programmable calculator.
    • One hand-written A4 formula sheet, both sides. This must be your own handwriting — printed or photocopied sheets are not allowed.
    • Student ID card.

    The formula-sheet rule is deliberate: writing it forces you to review every formula, and consulting it during the exam saves you the risk of a sign error.

    Grade boundaries¶

    Grade Range Description
    A 90–100 Excellent — full understanding, no significant errors
    A− 85–89 Very good — minor errors only
    B+ 80–84 Good — occasional errors, strong overall
    B 75–79 Solid — some conceptual gaps but correct approach
    B− 70–74 Adequate — noticeable gaps in understanding
    C+ 65–69 Fair — several conceptual errors
    C 60–64 Satisfactory — meets minimum requirements
    F < 60 Fail — does not meet requirements

    Final grades are not curved. The thresholds above are the standard ones for the MSc program and are not adjusted after the fact.

    Late work and extensions¶

    Late policy¶

    • Assignments: 10% of the maximum mark per calendar day (including weekends), capped at 3 days. After 3 days, the submission scores zero.
    • Examinations: no late sitting. Missing an examination without documented cause scores zero.

    Extensions¶

    Extensions are granted only for documented medical, family, or other exceptional circumstances:

    1. Submit a request before the deadline, with documentation (medical certificate, family emergency record).
    2. The instructor will grant an extension of typically 3–7 days, proportional to the disruption.
    3. If the deadline is missed due to a sudden emergency, contact the instructor within 48 hours of the deadline with documentation.

    Unsubstantiated requests are declined. Extensions are never granted retroactively except for a confirmed emergency.

    Make-up examinations¶

    • Mid-semester: students with documented medical or family emergencies may sit a make-up examination in week 10. The content and format are the same as the original; the maximum mark is capped at the class median of the original exam (standard practice to remove the "second chance" advantage).
    • Final: governed by the university's rules for deferred examinations. Application is made to the Registrar's Office, with medical documentation, within 5 working days of the missed exam.

    Academic integrity¶

    All submitted work (assignments, exams) must be your own.

    What is allowed¶

    • Discussing the concepts and methods with classmates — this is encouraged.
    • Consulting textbooks, lecture notes, and online references (cite them).
    • Using standard libraries (numpy, scipy, matplotlib) as permitted by the assignment brief.
    • Comparing your final answers with classmates after both have submitted — to check your reasoning.

    What is not allowed¶

    • Copying code or text from another student, past submissions, or an online source.
    • Sharing your code or write-up with classmates before the deadline.
    • Using AI tools to generate code or text that you present as your own (see below).
    • Re-submitting work submitted in another course.

    On AI tools¶

    Large language models (ChatGPT, Claude, GitHub Copilot, etc.) are powerful learning tools but cannot be used to generate submitted work. Permitted uses:

    • Explaining a concept you don't understand.
    • Debugging your own code (show the code, ask "why is this wrong?").
    • Checking a derivation you have already done.

    Not permitted: pasting an AI-generated answer or code as your submission. If in doubt, ask the instructor before submitting.

    Consequences¶

    Confirmed plagiarism or unauthorised AI use results in:

    • First offence: zero on the assessment component, and a written warning on file.
    • Second offence: zero on the assessment component and a report to the Dean's office, which may lead to academic probation or expulsion.

    All submissions are screened with plagiarism-detection tools. Code is additionally screened for AI-generated signatures.

    Feedback and grades¶

    • Assignment feedback: returned within 2 weeks of the submission deadline. Feedback is delivered through the LMS, with comments on each rubric criterion and a numeric mark.
    • Examination grades: posted within 3 weeks of the exam date.
    • Grade queries: if you believe a mark is incorrect, email the instructor within 1 week of receiving the result, with a written justification (what you expected, what you received, and why). Regrade requests that result in a lower mark are not reversed.
