    Schedule¶

    Week-by-week plan for ECEG-6201, 18-week semester (including revision and final-examination weeks).

    Reading before each lecture

    The Reading column in the detailed breakdown below lists the sections to read before the lecture — approximately 45–60 minutes of preparation per lecture. The lectures assume you have at least skimmed the section; they focus on worked examples, derivations, and computer demonstrations, not on restating the text.

    Detail by week¶

    Week 1 — Chapter 1, Part 1¶

    • Lecture. Characterization of minima; local vs. global vs. Pareto; gradient and Hessian; slope and curvature.
    • Reading. Chapter 1, §1.1–§1.5.
    • Preparation. Review matrix algebra (eigenvalues, positive definiteness).

    Week 2 — Chapter 1, Part 2¶

    • Lecture. Optimality conditions (unconstrained, equality, inequality, KKT); line-search subproblem; bracketing and golden-section; numerically vs. analytically derived gradients; adjoint variables.
    • Tutorial 1. Worked examples 1.1–1.13, gradient check, golden-section implementation.
    • Reading. Chapter 1, §1.6–§1.13.
    • Assignment 1 released: gradient checks, line search, adjoint derivation.

    Week 3 — Chapter 2, Part 1¶

    • Lecture. Direct-search methods (pattern search, Nelder–Mead); second-order methods (Newton); damped Newton.
    • Reading. Chapter 2, §2.1–§2.3.

    Week 4 — Chapter 2, Part 2¶

    • Lecture. Quasi-Newton (BFGS); sums of squares; Gauss–Newton; Levenberg–Marquardt.
    • Tutorial 2. BFGS update by hand; GN and LM on the exponential fit.
    • Reading. Chapter 2, §2.4–§2.8.
    • Assignment 1 due; Assignment 2 released: implement Newton, BFGS, and LM.

    Week 5 — Chapter 3, Part 1¶

    • Lecture. Penalty functions; logarithmic barrier; conditioning and duality-gap analysis; Slater's condition.
    • Reading. Chapter 3, §3.1–§3.3.

    Week 6 — Chapter 3, Part 2¶

    • Lecture. Augmented Lagrangian (method of multipliers); quadratic programming with equality constraints (KKT system); active-set preview.
    • Tutorial 3. Penalty convergence, AL iteration, QP by KKT.
    • Reading. Chapter 3, §3.4–§3.7.
    • Assignment 2 due; Assignment 3 released: penalty, AL, and QP.

    Week 7 — Review and mid-semester preparation¶

    • Lecture. Review of Chapters 1–3; worked exam-style problems; common pitfalls.
    • Tutorial. Q&A session; past mid-semester papers.
    • Reading. Review all of Chapters 1–3.
    • Assignment 3 due.

    Week 8 — Mid-semester examination¶

    • Exam. 2 hours, closed book, 30% of final grade. Covers Chapters 1–3.
    • No lecture this week.

    Week 9 — Chapter 4, Part 1¶

    • Lecture. Random (stochastic) processes; strict-sense and wide-sense stationarity; autocorrelation; cross-correlation; ergodicity preview.
    • Reading. Chapter 4, §4.1–§4.2.
    • Assignment 4 released: WSS checks, random-walk moments.

    Week 10 — Chapter 4, Part 2¶

    • Lecture. Poisson point processes; shot noise and Campbell's theorem; cyclostationarity; band-limited processes and the sampling theorem; matched filter and entropy.
    • Tutorial 4. Shot-noise mean/variance, cyclostationarity of PAM, Nyquist-rate sampling.
    • Reading. Chapter 4, §4.3–§4.8.
    • Assignment 4 due; Assignment 5 released: shot noise, matched filter, sampling.

    Week 11 — Chapter 5, Part 1¶

    • Lecture. Wiener–Khinchin; PSD; Karhunen–Loève expansion; spectral factorization and the innovations representation.
    • Reading. Chapter 5, §5.1–§5.3.

    Week 12 — Chapter 5, Part 2¶

    • Lecture. Ergodicity; periodogram and Welch's method; LMMSE and orthogonality principle; Wiener filter; Kalman filter.
    • Tutorial 5. Periodogram variance, LMMSE, causal Wiener, Kalman filter.
    • Reading. Chapter 5, §5.4–§5.9.
    • Assignment 5 due; Assignment 6 released: LMMSE, Wiener, Kalman.

    Week 13 — Chapter 6, Part 1¶

    • Lecture. Markov property; DTMC; transition matrix; Chapman–Kolmogorov; classification of states.
    • Reading. Chapter 6, §6.1–§6.3.

    Week 14 — Chapter 6, Part 2¶

    • Lecture. Stationary distribution; ergodicity; detailed balance; CTMC and generator matrix; birth–death chains.
    • Tutorial 6. Classification; stationary distribution by power iteration and eigen-decomposition; CTMC.
    • Reading. Chapter 6, §6.4–§6.7.
    • Assignment 6 due; Assignment 7 released: Markov-chain analysis.

    Week 15 — Chapter 7, Part 1¶

    • Lecture. Kendall notation; Little's formula; birth–death framework; M/M/1 queue and performance measures; waiting-time distribution; Burke's theorem.
    • Reading. Chapter 7, §7.1–§7.4.

    Week 16 — Chapter 7, Part 2¶

    • Lecture. M/M/s queue; Erlang-C; M/M/1/K; PK formula; Jackson networks; simulation.
    • Tutorial 7. Erlang-C staffing problem, M/M/1/K, discrete-event simulation.
    • Reading. Chapter 7, §7.5–§7.9.
    • Assignment 7 due.

    Week 17 — Revision¶

    • Lecture. Review of Chapters 4–7; worked exam-style problems.
    • Tutorial. Q&A session; past final-exam papers.

    Week 18 — Final examination¶

    • Exam. 3 hours, closed book, 50% of final grade. Covers the whole syllabus, weighted toward Chapters 4–7.

    Policies¶

    Deadlines and attendance

    • Late assignments lose 10% per calendar day; after 3 days they score zero.
    • Mid-semester and final examinations are closed book; a non-programmable calculator is permitted.
    • Missing the mid-semester requires documented medical or family-emergency evidence; a make-up exam will be scheduled in week 10.
    • Attendance at tutorials is strongly encouraged — they are where most of the examinable worked examples are covered.

    Holidays

    National and religious holidays that fall on a lecture day will be announced on the Home page and, where possible, rescheduled. Check the announcements page each week.
