    ECEG-6201 — Analytical Optimization and Stochastic Processes¶

    MSc in Communication Engineering · Year 1, Semester I

    Welcome to the course website. This site hosts the complete lecture notes, worked examples, computational implementations (Python / Java / C#), quizzes, and tutorial problems for the two halves of this course: optimization (Chapters 1–3) and stochastic processes (Chapters 4–7).

    What you will learn¶

    • Formulate and solve optimization problems

      Recognize an optimization problem, characterize its minima, and choose a gradient / Newton / quasi-Newton / penalty method appropriate to its smoothness, dimension, and cost per evaluation.

    • Model and analyze random phenomena

      Work with random processes, correlations, spectra, and mean-square estimation. Apply stochastic-process tools to communication-engineering systems.

    • Analyze Markov chains and queues

      Classify a Markov chain, compute its stationary distribution, and derive the performance measures of elementary queuing systems.

    Chapters¶

    • Optimization — Preliminaries

      Minima, gradients, Hessians, optimality conditions (KKT), line search, numerical vs. analytic gradients, adjoint variables.

      Chapter 1

    • Unconstrained Optimization

      Direct search (Nelder–Mead, pattern search), Newton, quasi-Newton (BFGS), Gauss–Newton, Levenberg–Marquardt.

      Chapter 2

    • Constrained Optimization

      Penalty and barrier functions, augmented Lagrangian, quadratic programming (KKT system, active-set preview).

      Chapter 3

    • Stochastic Processes & Random Walks

      WSS vs. SSS, correlations, random walks, Poisson points, shot noise, cyclostationarity, sampling, matched filter, entropy.

      Chapter 4

    • Spectral Representation & Estimation

      Wiener–Khinchin, Karhunen–Loève, spectral factorization, ergodicity, periodogram/Welch, LMMSE, Wiener filter, Kalman filter.

      Chapter 5

    • Markov Chains

      DTMC, classification (recurrent/transient/periodic), stationary distribution, ergodicity, CTMC and generator matrix.

      Chapter 6

    • Introduction to Queuing Theory

      Kendall notation, Little's formula, M/M/1, M/M/s (Erlang-C), M/M/1/K, Pollaczek–Khinchine, Jackson networks, discrete-event simulation.

      Chapter 7

    How to use this site¶

    • Read in order

      Chapters are designed to be read in sequence — each builds on the previous. Chapter 1 lays the mathematical foundations for optimization; Chapter 4 introduces the probabilistic tools that Chapters 5–7 build on.

    • Check your understanding

      Every chapter has a three-part quiz (MCQ, fill-in, define/explain) with an inline answer key. The tutorial problems at the end of each chapter are extra exercises; solutions are in the Solutions page.

    • Run the code

      Every chapter has Python, Java, and C# implementations of its main algorithms. Copy the snippets, run them, and compare against the analytical answers worked out by hand.

    • See the schedule

      The Schedule page has the week-by-week plan: lecture topics, tutorial sessions, reading assignments, and assessment dates.

    Recommended preparation¶

    No prior background in optimization or stochastic processes is assumed, but the following will make the course much easier:

    • Linear algebra — matrix algebra, eigenvalues, positive-definite matrices
    • Calculus — partial derivatives, gradients, Taylor expansion
    • Probability — random variables, expectation, variance, common distributions
    • Python — NumPy and SciPy are used throughout for the computational demos (Java and C# equivalents are also provided for students from those backgrounds)

    If any of these feels shaky, review Boyd & Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, Appendices A–C (free PDF online) or Papoulis & Pillai, Chapters 1–5 before the first lecture. Both are linked from the Resources page.
